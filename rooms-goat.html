The small things (5)
  • The footbridge to Luna The pedestrian span Goat Island throws to its little sister. Walkable here; in person it is a short crossing with water on both sides of it.
  • The rock pile below the ledge Boulders the falls tore off its own lip, and the ones the engineers left when they cleared the face in 1969. A falls into a rock pile is a shallower fall; this one measures its drop to the boulders, not to the river.
  • More of the pile The talus runs the length of the pool below the American side.
  • Terrapin Point The stone shelf at Goat Island's north end — the closest the United States comes to the Horseshoe. The point has been rebuilt twice after rockfalls and one ice storm; what you stand on is as much engineering as geology.
  • The rail at Terrapin Iron on stone, the same kit the Canadian parapet uses; the two banks standardised on keeping people back from the brink long ago.

The American bank seen from its own island, at the strip's measure: the American Falls' 290 m crest and 21-30 m drop onto the 1969 boulders, Luna Island's 350 ft of dolostone and its 1954-72 closure, the Horseshoe's 57 m and 670 m read from the Canadian record, the Rainbow's dates — sources. The island's walk line, the banks' woodlines, every height not named, and the gap-bridges between 290-metre faces and a 160-metre lane: drawn, and said so. The falls here are not miniatures: they are the same two curtains the Canadian page stands at, seen from the other bank's side of the water.
